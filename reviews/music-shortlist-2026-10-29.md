# Hairpin: music shortlist (2026-10-29)

Search and read only. Nothing was downloaded. **I could not listen to anything**, so the chapter fit is judged from album liner notes, instrumentation and track titles. Audition every pick before you commit to it.

## The honest headline

**I found no real Khasi or Northeast Indian folk recordings under a usable licence.**
- Archive.org has 8 "Khasi" items under CC BY 3.0/4.0, but they are spoken poetry readings (Janet Hujon reading her translation of Soso Tham, Open Book Publishers), not music. Example: https://archive.org/details/KaIingIMei-Home (CC BY 4.0, 5:10).
- The IGNCA field recordings on archive.org (`dni.ncaa.*`) are CC BY-NC 4.0, but none are Khasi. Freesound returns 0 results for "meghalaya" and nothing usable for "shillong".
- Pixabay, Jamendo and Bandcamp's creative-commons tag have nothing for Khasi, duitara or Meghalaya. Pixabay "India" music is generic sitar/tabla/"Krishna flute": that is the kitsch you want to avoid.
- The authentic route is permission, not a licence. Da Minot (Shillong; duitara, bom, bamboo flutes) and the duitara player Banshailang Mukhim (Shlem Institute, Smit) are both active. A short email asking to use one piece in a non-commercial portfolio piece, with credit, is realistic. That is the only way to get a real duitara.

So the plan below is the closest **real, acoustic, recorded** alternative. Its folk colour comes from plucked strings rather than from region.

## The spine: Blue Dot Sessions (one studio, one sound)

Blue Dot Sessions is a New Orleans studio that records improvised, minimalist acoustic music with live players (not sample libraries). Building most of the score from 2-3 of their albums makes it sound like one composer.
- **Resolute** (2018): *"Classical guitar and pizzicato strings, melodies and textures"*. Plucked nylon strings plus plucked violins is the nearest thing to a duitara bed. This is the main album.
- **Origami** (2015): *"marimba and cello… bowing marimba and field recordings"*. Wood and breath textures for the uneasy chapters.
- **Ocean Blooms** (2025): *"Chamber strings slowly spreading out to fill the room."* For the grave and blooming chapters.

### Blue Dot Sessions licence (verified)
- **Licence:** CC BY-NC 4.0 (Attribution-NonCommercial 4.0 International). Shown on each FMA album page and on the track page for Roundpine, which I checked. Every album page also says "check individual tracks" (FMA boilerplate).
- **Attribution:** the FMA album pages ask: *Please credit as "Blue Dot Sessions" with a link to https://www.sessions.blue*. Suggested credit: `"<Track>" by Blue Dot Sessions (sessions.blue), CC BY-NC 4.0`.
- **Embedding in a website:** CC BY-NC allows copying, adapting and sharing for non-commercial purposes, so hosting trimmed loops inside the page is fine. Beyond NC there is no separate no-redistribution clause.
- **The FLAG:** sessions.blue/licensing says the free tier covers *"non-monetized projects that aren't being made as part of your job."* A personal portfolio piece made to apply for a job fits that reading. If Hairpin is a paid assignment or take-home *for* Pocket FM, it arguably does not, and you would buy a project licence (sliding scale, no published price). Decide which applies before you ship.
- **Preview:** each FMA track page has a play button (rendered by JavaScript; my text fetch could not see the player itself). sessions.blue has a full browsable player at https://app.sessions.blue/browse.

## Clean-licence fallback: Incompetech (Kevin MacLeod)
Use this if the NC clause is a problem. Kevin MacLeod's free tier is CC BY 4.0. The licence page says only "Creative Commons — Free… requires that you credit the music", and the exact CC version and credit line are generated per track, so **verify on each track page**. I could not load the track pages: they render with JavaScript.
- Usual credit: `"Title" Kevin MacLeod (incompetech.com), Licensed under Creative Commons: By Attribution 4.0, http://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/`
- CC BY 4.0 allows commercial use and embedding in a website, with no no-redistribution clause.
- Instrumentation is from Incompetech's pieces.json.
- **Caveats:**
  - Most of MacLeod's catalogue is made with virtual instruments, not live players. That works against your "real recorded" brief.
  - The music is heavily used online and may feel familiar.
- Other matches: "River Flute" (flute, synth, river; 20:28; Calming, Mystical) for ch.5. "Almost Bliss" (guitars, percussion; 5:17) for ch.3.
